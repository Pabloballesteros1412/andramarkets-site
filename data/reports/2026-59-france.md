## Executive Summary

**Key figures**

- **119.0%**: public debt to GDP, Q2 2026, a record (INSEE)
- **5.1%**: deficit to GDP in 2025, vs 2.9% in the euro area
- **4.96%**: 10-year OAT peak on 1 Oct 2026, highest since 2002
- **−0.6%**: CAC 40 since the June 2024 dissolution; DAX +38.1%

France has the euro area’s largest public spending, one of its largest deficits and a debt that has risen by about 20 points of GDP since 2019. Since the snap election of June 2024, two prime ministers have fallen and a third has resigned, three agencies have cut the rating to A+, and in 2026 France began to borrow at higher rates than Italy.

**Our view:** this is a slow-moving fiscal problem turned into a market problem by politics. There is no funding crisis, but the State’s interest bill now exceeds the armed forces budget, and the 2027 election leaves little room for correction.

## The Debt Trajectory

France has not balanced a budget since 1974. Its debt, just over 20% of GDP in the early 1980s, reached 98% in 2019, jumped during the pandemic and kept rising afterwards, unlike in Italy, Spain or Portugal. In the second quarter of 2026 it hit a record 119.0% of GDP, or €3.6tn.

Sources: Eurostat (annual, 1995 to 2025); INSEE (Q2 2026); Banque de France (1980s and last balanced budget).

## The Deficit

The 2025 deficit came in at 5.1% of GDP, better than forecast but, according to the Cour des comptes, thanks to higher taxes rather than spending restraint. It is now expected at 5.4% in 2026. The 2027 budget aims for 5.0% with €54bn of measures; the fiscal council calls its growth assumptions optimistic.

- Portugal: +0.7%
- Spain: −2.4%
- Germany: −2.7%
- Euro area: −2.9%
- Italy: −3.1%
- France: −5.1%

Source: Eurostat (April 2026 notification). 2026 and 2027 figures from the French government and the Haut Conseil des finances publiques.

## Spending and Taxes

Public spending reached 57.2% of GDP in 2025, the highest in the euro area and 7.4 points above its average. The Banque de France attributes about two-thirds of the gap to social protection, with pensions alone worth 2.2 points. Compulsory levies take 43.6% of GDP, so the deficit cannot easily be closed through taxes; the 2023 pension reform has been suspended until 2028.

**Government expenditure, 2025 (% of GDP)**

- France: 57.2%
- Italy: 51.2%
- Germany: 50.5%
- Euro area: 49.8%
- Spain: 45.3%

Sources: Eurostat; INSEE; Banque de France (comparison with euro-area peers, 2023 data).

## The Cost of Debt

Higher rates are feeding through. General government interest rose 11% in 2025 to €64.7bn (INSEE), or 2.2% of GDP, up from 1.3% in 2020. In the 2027 budget bill the State’s interest bill, €72.9bn, exceeds the armed forces budget. The Cour des comptes expects interest of more than €100bn a year by 2029.

**Interest paid by general government (% of GDP)**

- 2020: 1.3%
- 2021: 1.4%
- 2022: 1.9%
- 2023: 1.9%
- 2024: 2.0%
- 2025: 2.2%

Sources: Eurostat; INSEE; 2027 budget bill (PLF 2027) as reported; Cour des comptes (June 2026).

## The Bond Market

The extra yield investors demand on French ten-year bonds over German Bunds was about 50bp before the June 2024 dissolution. It averaged 101bp in September 2026, the most since 2012. On 1 October the ten-year OAT yield touched 4.96%, its highest since 2002, and the spread reached 133bp (Reuters); intraday on 2 October it passed 150bp, the widest since 2011 (Bloomberg).

On the monthly-average OAT-Bund ten-year spread, the euro-crisis peak was 154bp in November 2011.

Source: OECD long-term interest rates via FRED (monthly averages); Andra Markets calculation. Daily data from Reuters.

## France and Southern Europe

For a decade France borrowed far more cheaply than southern Europe. In November 2024 its ten-year yield briefly matched Greece’s; in September 2025 it matched Italy’s; and since late August 2026 it has been above Italy’s, by a record 30bp on 9 October, as reported. Spain now borrows more cheaply than France.

Sources: OECD via FRED (monthly averages; Italy to August 2026); Bloomberg, ANSA and press reports for daily crossings.

## Credit Ratings

Fitch and S&P cut France to A+ in the autumn of 2025, and Scope followed in September 2026. Moody’s, the last of the large agencies at the AA level, has France on negative outlook and reviews it on 23 October.

## Political Instability

Since the June 2024 dissolution produced a hung parliament, no government has had a majority. The 2026 budget was passed only in February, without a vote, and the 2027 budget is again in difficulty. Marine Le Pen’s appeal court ruling in July left her free to run in April 2027, pending a ruling by the Cour de cassation, and she leads first-round polls.

- **Jun 2024**: snap election; hung parliament
- **Dec 2024**: Barnier government falls to a censure motion
- **Sep 2025**: Bayrou loses a confidence vote, 364 to 194
- **Oct 2025**: Lecornu resigns and is reappointed four days later
- **Feb 2026**: 2026 budget adopted by Article 49.3
- **Jul 2026**: Le Pen appeal ruling; she declares her candidacy
- **Oct 2026**: committee rejects revenue part of the 2027 budget
- **Apr 2027**: presidential election, first round

Sources: Bloomberg; Al Jazeera; Public Sénat; polling compilations (secondary sources).

## Investors and Issuance

France has no difficulty selling its bonds, but it must sell more of them and to more price-sensitive buyers. The Treasury plans a record €340bn of medium and long-term issuance in 2027, while the Eurosystem is reducing its holdings. ECB President Lagarde said on 14 October that “the primary and first solution lies at home”, pushing back on talk of central-bank support.

- **Issuance programme, 2026**: €310bn
- **Issuance programme, 2027**: €340bn, a record
- **Held by non-residents**: 57.5% (Q1 2026, market value)
- **French credit institutions**: 10.5% of negotiable State debt (Q1 2026)
- **ECB deposit rate**: 2.50%
- **EU excessive deficit procedure**: open since Jul 2024; target below 3% by 2029

Sources: Agence France Trésor (issuance; holdings, monthly bulletin); Bloomberg; ECB; European Commission.

## Equities

The CAC 40 is back where it was before the dissolution, while Madrid, Milan and Frankfurt have gained between 38% and 70%. Banks have suffered as sovereign spreads widened, and luxury, a large part of the index, has collapsed: LVMH is down 38% this year and Kering 28%. TotalEnergies, up 45% on oil, is the exception.

## The Real Economy

The economy has stalled. GDP fell 0.2% in the first quarter of 2026 and was flat in the second, as the energy shock from the Middle East war pushed inflation above 3%. Households keep saving about 17% of their income, a sign of low confidence rather than a lack of resources.

- **GDP growth, Q1 / Q2 2026**: −0.2% / 0.0% q/q
- **GDP forecast 2026 / 2027 (Banque de France)**: 0.4% / 0.9%
- **Unemployment rate, Q2 2026**: 8.3%
- **Inflation, Sep 2026 (CPI / HICP)**: 3.1% / 3.4%
- **Household savings rate, Q2 2026**: 17.2%
- **Business failures, last 12 months**: ~72,000 (Altares)

Sources: INSEE; Banque de France (September 2026 projections); Altares as reported by DAF Magazine.

## Verdict

**Not a debt crisis, but a slow loss of credibility.** France can fund itself; the question is at what price, and who will cut the deficit before 2027.

- **Moody’s rating**: Aa3 negative; next test: review, 23 Oct
- **2027 budget**: in Assembly; next test: first vote ~20 Oct
- **OAT-Bund spread**: ~135-140bp; next test: new high above 2 Oct peak
- **S&P rating**: A+ stable; next test: review, 27 Nov
- **Presidential election**: Le Pen leads polls; next test: Apr 2027
