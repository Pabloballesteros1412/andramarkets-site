## Executive Summary

**The market traded the guide, not the quarter.** Intel beat on revenue, margin and EPS for an eighth straight quarter. What it could not do was show the improvement continuing: revenue, margin and EPS all step down in the fourth quarter, and management warned that PC weakness runs at least through the first half of 2027.

**Two problems sit behind the guide.** PC demand is soft, with client revenue down 5%, and Intel cannot make enough server chips: supply constraints in data centre, an Israel plant shutdown costing about $300 million and roughly two points of margin, and higher memory costs that raise PC prices and hurt demand.

**The sell-off spread through the PC and memory chain.** AMD, Qualcomm and Arm, which share Intel’s exposure to PCs, fell 9% to 12%. Memory names fell on fears that pricing is peaking, despite SanDisk reporting revenue up 378% and an 8-for-1 split. Equipment makers Applied Materials, Lam and ASML fell 6% to 7%.

**It landed on an already nervous market.** Amazon’s −$42 billion trailing free cash flow, Alphabet’s first negative free-cash-flow quarters and a 3.6% PCE inflation print had already raised the question of who pays for AI and at what interest rate. Intel added a third question: what happens to the rest of the chip market while AI absorbs supply and capital.

**Data centre demand is not the problem.** Intel’s server business grew 78% and management says it cannot meet demand for Xeon. The fall reflects how far the shares had run, from a 52-week low of $32.89 to $142.35, and how little room that left for a sequential decline.

**Our reading.** Friday was a positioning event inside a durable AI cycle rather than a verdict on it. The question for November is breadth: whether AI demand can carry the parts of the semiconductor market that depend on PCs, phones and memory pricing, at a time when rates are rising and the market is paying for cash flow, not promises.

## Intel’s Quarter

Intel’s third quarter was its best in years by most measures. Revenue rose 21% to $16.5 billion, GAAP gross margin reached 43.0% and non-GAAP 44.1%, up four points, and the non-GAAP operating margin rose to 19.6% from 11.2%. Data Center and AI revenue grew 78% to $7.3 billion, up 17% sequentially, and is now almost as large as the PC business, whose revenue fell 5% to $8.1 billion. Intel Foundry revenue rose 58% to $6.7 billion including internal sales, but the unit still lost $1.85 billion. Operating cash flow was $5.1 billion. Chief financial officer David Zinsner called it the strongest data centre growth in 15 years.

Source: Intel Q3 2026 earnings release (8-K). Foundry includes intersegment revenue and does not add to the group total.

The guidance changed the story. Intel expects fourth-quarter revenue of $15.0 to 16.0 billion, a midpoint of $15.5 billion against a consensus near $17.1 billion, with some forecasts above $18 billion, according to Bloomberg. Non-GAAP EPS is guided to $0.25 against $0.43 expected, and non-GAAP gross margin to about 38%, six points below the third quarter. Management cited PC weakness lasting at least through the first half of 2027, data centre supply constraints, the Israel plant shutdown and investment in capacity and working capital, and gave no segment guidance.

Sources: Intel Q4 2026 outlook; consensus via Bloomberg (revenue ~$17.08B, EPS $0.43) reported by Quartz and Yahoo Finance.

The size of the fall reflects positioning as much as news. Intel traded at $32.89 at its 52-week low and $142.35 at its high; it closed Thursday at $92.07 and Friday at $65.76, on volume of about 448 million shares, more than four times the three-month average. A stock priced for continuous improvement met a guide that showed a pause, and holders who had made multiples of their money sold at once.

## Contagion

The sell-off followed the supply chain. Companies with direct exposure to PCs fell hardest: AMD about 11%, Qualcomm 11% and Arm 10% intraday. Memory followed: SanDisk lost 13.5% despite beating forecasts and announcing an 8-for-1 split, as Wells Fargo cut targets and investors questioned whether memory pricing is near a peak; SK Hynix fell 8% and Micron 5%. The reason connects back to Intel’s warning: AI demand is driving memory prices up, which raises PC costs and hurts PC demand, which in turn hurts the CPU and memory makers that depend on PCs. AI is absorbing supply, capital and pricing power from the rest of the chain. Equipment makers fell 6% to 7% and TSMC 6%.

Nvidia fell least among the large chip names, about 4%, but enough for its market value to slip below $5 trillion and for Apple, which fell only 1.2%, to retake first place. Broadcom lost 5.5%. Nvidia reports in the second half of November, and its numbers will test whether the AI-specific part of the chain is as strong as the Amazon, Alphabet and Microsoft cloud results suggest.

## The Macro Backdrop

The day did not happen in a vacuum. On Thursday the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, the PCE price index, rose 3.6% year on year in September with core at 3.1%, after the Fed held rates at 3.75% to 4% on 28 October with two officials voting for a hike. On Friday St. Louis Fed president Alberto Musalem said further tightening will be needed “soon” and Dallas Fed president Lorie Logan argued for at least two more quarter-point hikes. Futures priced roughly a 70% to 80% chance of a December hike. The ten-year Treasury yield reached 5.31% on 5 October, its highest since 2002. Higher discount rates hurt long-duration growth stocks most, and the semiconductors had led the market up.

Not everything fell. On Thursday nine of eleven S&P sectors rose, with health care and real estate up more than 2%, and on Friday Exxon and Chevron touched record highs after strong results. The rotation is consistent with a market moving from growth to cash flow and from long duration to defensive and energy exposure, rather than a broad liquidation.

## What It Means

**Guidance has become the main event.** Intel, Amazon and Alphabet all beat or met on the quarter and were sold on the outlook or the cash flow. With valuations high, the market is paying for the next quarter, not the last one.

**AI demand is narrowing the winners.** Server CPUs, accelerators and high-bandwidth memory are short of supply; PCs, phones and parts of memory face higher costs and softer demand. The same cycle that lifts one part of a company can squeeze another, as Intel showed within one release.

**Rates are the swing factor for multiples.** A December Fed hike is largely priced; a second or a 50-basis-point move would hit long-duration tech hardest. The jobs report on 6 November and the CPI in mid-November are the next tests.
