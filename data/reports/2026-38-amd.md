**Coverage summary.** Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) reported second-quarter results on 4 August. Revenue rose 50 per cent to a record $11.5 billion and data centre revenue more than doubled to $6.7 billion. It guided third-quarter revenue to about $13.0 billion, well above expectations, and said data centre revenue should more than double again in 2027 as its new MI450 AI chips and Helios rack systems ramp for OpenAI, Meta and others. The shares fell 7 per cent the day after the results but have since risen to a record, passing a market value of $1 trillion on 21 September, and closed at $645.86 on 7 October, roughly three times their level at the start of the year. ## Executive Summary

**The AI business is arriving.** AMD has spent years as the distant second supplier of AI chips. With the MI450 generation and its Helios rack, which combines 72 GPUs with its own processors, it now has multi-gigawatt commitments from OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic. Management expects data centre AI revenue to grow “well over 100 per cent” in 2027 and earnings per share to come in significantly above the $20 target set at its 2025 analyst day.

**Processors keep gaining share.** AMD’s EPYC server processors held a record 46 per cent of server processor revenue in the first quarter, according to Mercury Research, and demand for general-purpose computing in AI data centres is growing. Server revenue is expected to grow 70 per cent or more next year.

**The valuation leaves no margin for error.** At 58 times forward earnings, AMD is the most expensive company in this series, and the share price is above the analysts’ average target. The shares fell 7 per cent after a quarter that beat on every measure, a sign of how much is already expected.

## Business Overview

AMD designs processors (CPUs) for servers and PCs, graphics processors and AI accelerators (GPUs), and programmable chips for industrial and other uses; like Nvidia, it outsources manufacturing to TSMC. It reports four segments: Data Center, which includes EPYC server processors and Instinct AI accelerators and now generates 58 per cent of revenue; Client, its Ryzen PC processors; Gaming, mainly chips for game consoles; and Embedded, built on the acquisition of Xilinx.

## Second-Quarter Results

Revenue of $11.54 billion beat the consensus of $11.28 billion and non-GAAP earnings per share of $1.66 beat $1.62. The non-GAAP gross margin was 56 per cent and the operating margin 27 per cent. Third-quarter guidance of about $13.0 billion, plus or minus $300 million, was well above the $12.52 billion expected and implies growth of about 41 per cent; analysts now expect about $13.2 billion. Management warned that early production of Helios systems could weigh on gross margins while yields improve.

## The AI Customers

AMD’s AI strategy rests on a few very large agreements. OpenAI agreed in October 2025 to deploy 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs, starting with 1 gigawatt of MI450 in the second half of 2026, and Meta signed a similar agreement for up to 6 gigawatts in February 2026; both received warrants to buy up to 160 million AMD shares, which vest as deployments and share price targets are reached. Anthropic plans up to 2 gigawatts from the first half of 2027, and Microsoft and Oracle will also deploy Helios. AMD expects these deals to generate tens of billions of dollars of revenue.

Source: AMD announcements (October 2025, February 2026) and Q2 2026 results. Deployment begins in the second half of 2026 (OpenAI, Meta) and first half of 2027 (Anthropic).

## Valuation

At $645.86 AMD is worth about $1.05 trillion, or 165 times trailing earnings and 58 times forward earnings. The average target of the 55 analysts covered by StockAnalysis, $636.21, is below the current price, although several brokers have raised theirs recently, including Citi to $800 and Bank of America to $720. The bull case is that AMD reaches its long-term goals, including earnings per share above $20, within a few years, which would bring the multiple down quickly; the bear case is that any delay in the MI450 ramp would leave the shares exposed.

## Risk Considerations

The first risk is execution: Helios is AMD’s first rack-scale system, and delays or low yields would hit both revenue and margins at the moment expectations are highest. Second, the AI pipeline depends on a few customers, some of which rely on external funding, and the warrants granted to OpenAI and Meta could dilute shareholders. Third, Nvidia remains far larger and is launching its Rubin generation at the same time. Finally, export rules exclude China from guidance, and higher memory costs pressure margins.

## Outlook and Monitoring Points

AMD reports third-quarter results on 3 November after the US market closes. Investors will watch whether revenue reaches $13 billion, the first revenue from MI450 and Helios, the gross margin as the ramp begins, and any update on 2027. With the shares above the average target, a merely good report may not be enough.
