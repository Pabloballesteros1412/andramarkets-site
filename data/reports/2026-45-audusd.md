## Executive Summary

**Momentum and trend disagree.** Twelve-month momentum is strongly positive (+9.8 per cent), but the price has fallen below its moving averages in the past month, with an RSI of 31, at the edge of oversold territory. This kind of conflict usually marks a consolidation rather than a new trend.

**Volatility at a ten-year low.** At 4.8 per cent, one-month realised volatility is below every reading of the past ten years except about 1 per cent of days. Very low volatility in a currency that usually moves with risk appetite is unusual and leaves it exposed to a shock.

**A risk currency.** AUD/USD rises with US equities (+0.39) and falls with the VIX (−0.34), among the strongest links to risk appetite in this series. Its direction in the coming weeks depends more on global markets than on the RBA.

## Price and Trend

AUD/USD rose from 0.6445 in November 2025 to 0.7257 on 7 May, its highest level in this period, as the RBA began raising rates. It then moved sideways and, since early September, has fallen below the 50-day average (0.7091) and the 200-day (0.7030). The z-score against its one-year average is close to zero (+0.1), so the currency is neither cheap nor expensive relative to its recent history.

## Volatility

One-month realised volatility of 4.8 per cent compares with a ten-year median of 8.7 per cent. Three-month volatility is 5.6 per cent and one-year volatility 7.7 per cent, so the calm is recent. It coincides with low volatility in equities (the VIX was about 15 on 7 October), which supports the idea that the Australian dollar is reflecting a calm global market.

## Distribution of Returns

Daily returns over the past year are close to normal, with a skew of +0.12 and excess kurtosis of only 0.4. The largest rise was +1.74 per cent on 8 April, the day of the US-Iran ceasefire, and the largest fall −1.27 per cent on 15 May. The one-day value at risk at 95 per cent is −0.79 per cent.

## What Moves the Pair

The correlation of weekly changes with the S&P 500 is +0.39 and with the VIX −0.34, while the link with US two-year yields is close to zero (−0.10). The broad dollar explains most of the rest (−0.70). In other words, the RBA’s increases have not translated into a stronger currency because investors trade the Australian dollar mainly as a proxy for global growth and for China.

## Positioning and Forecasts

Speculators increased their net short position in the Australian dollar to 63,239 contracts in the week to 29 September, from 46,814. Forecasters are more positive: MUFG expects 0.70 at the end of the year and 0.73 by the third quarter of 2027, and Trading Economics’ model has 0.70 and 0.71. The gap between bearish positioning and constructive forecasts is one of the widest in this series.

## Risk Considerations and Calendar

The main risk is a fall in global equities or weaker Chinese demand. A softer US dollar after weak payrolls would support the currency. Key dates: RBA minutes on 13 October, US CPI on 14 October, Australian CPI on 28 October and the RBA on 3 November. The rate story supports the Aussie; the risk story will decide it.
