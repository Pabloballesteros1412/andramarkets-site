**Coverage summary.** ASML, based in Veldhoven in the Netherlands, is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, which are needed to print the most advanced chips made by TSMC, Samsung, Intel and the memory makers. In the second quarter, net sales rose 21 per cent to €9.3 billion and the gross margin reached 54 per cent, both above guidance. ASML raised its 2026 sales forecast to €43 to 45 billion from €36 to 40 billion and said low-NA EUV capacity for 2027 is close to fully covered by orders. The US-listed shares closed at $1,804.96 on 7 October, almost double their 52-week low but 10 per cent below their high, after two sharp falls linked to China and to doubts about AI spending. ## Executive Summary

**A much bigger 2026.** ASML now expects 2026 sales of €43 to 45 billion, about 16 per cent more at the midpoint than it guided in April, with EUV system sales up more than 45 per cent and sales to memory makers up more than 75 per cent as they build capacity for AI. The gross margin guidance was raised to 54 to 56 per cent.

**Next year is largely booked.** Chief financial officer Roger Dassen said that “for 2027, we are now close to being fully covered with orders for Low NA EUV”, and that a significant number of 2028 orders are already in. ASML plans to raise its capacity for low-NA EUV and immersion DUV machines by about 30 per cent in 2027 and is studying another 30 per cent for 2028.

**The shares are sensitive to two fears.** They fell about 7 per cent in late July after reports that a Chinese company is producing its own DUV machines, and 7 per cent on 14 September amid doubts about the pace of AI spending. At about 33 times forward earnings, ASML is the second most expensive company in this series.

## Business Overview

Lithography machines project the patterns of a chip’s circuits onto silicon wafers using light; the shorter the wavelength, the smaller the features they can print. ASML sells two main families. EUV machines, which use extreme ultraviolet light, are required for the most advanced processes and are made only by ASML; the newest High-NA versions print even finer features. DUV machines, which use deep ultraviolet light, are used for less advanced layers and older chips, where ASML competes with Nikon and Canon. A third business, installed base management, services and upgrades the thousands of machines already in factories and provides recurring revenue.

Source: ASML Q2 2026 results and investor call (15 July 2026). System sales were 51 per cent logic and 49 per cent memory.

## China and Export Controls

China was ASML’s largest market in 2024, at 36 per cent of sales, as Chinese chipmakers stockpiled machines ahead of export restrictions. Its share fell to 33 per cent in 2025 and is expected to be about 20 per cent in 2026. The risk has not disappeared: a bill introduced in the US Congress in April, the MATCH Act, would restrict sales of immersion DUV machines and their servicing to leading Chinese chipmakers and ask the Netherlands and Japan to align. It is not law, but Bank of America estimates that a full ban on DUV sales and servicing would cut ASML’s revenue by 14 to 15 per cent. Reports of Chinese companies building their own DUV machines are a longer-term threat.

Source: ASML, as reported by Caixin and The Standard (January 2026). 2026 is a company estimate.

## High-NA and the Customers

ASML expects to recognise revenue on four to five High-NA machines this year. Intel is the leading adopter and has printed more than a million wafers with them, while Samsung and SK hynix have installed their first units. TSMC has said it will not use High-NA for its A14 process, which limits demand in the near term. Memory makers are now among the largest customers: SK hynix disclosed a purchase of EUV machines worth about US$7.9 billion through 2027. ASML is returning cash through a €12 billion buyback programme for 2026 to 2028.

## Risk Considerations

Equipment makers are the most cyclical part of the chip industry: when chipmakers slow their investment, orders for new machines fall before chip sales do, and ASML’s 2025 experience, when it could not confirm growth for the following year, shows how quickly sentiment can turn. Today the risks are a slowdown in AI investment, new export restrictions on China, slower adoption of High-NA machines and the pace of investment by Intel and Samsung. Because ASML reports in euros while many peers and investors think in dollars, currency movements also affect the comparison.

## Outlook and Monitoring Points

ASML reports third-quarter results on 14 October, a day before TSMC. Investors will look for confirmation of the €43 to 45 billion forecast, any first indication of growth in 2027, comments on China and the MATCH Act, the number of High-NA machines shipped, and whether memory makers keep ordering. ASML’s comments on 2027 will be the clearest early signal of whether the AI investment cycle continues next year.
