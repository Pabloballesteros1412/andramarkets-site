## Executive Summary

**A steady, managed trend.** The pair is below its 200-day average by 1.7 per cent and twelve-month momentum is −5.7 per cent, the clearest downward trend for the dollar among the six pairs. The appreciation has been gradual, with no daily move larger than 0.6 per cent in the past year.

**The central bank is slowing the move.** Since 23 September the PBoC has set the daily fixing weaker than market estimates, signalling that it does not want a rapid appreciation while trade talks with the United States continue.

**Carry runs the other way.** Chinese two-year yields of 1.26 per cent are 3.53 points below US yields, so holding renminbi costs money. That limits speculative demand and helps explain why the PBoC can manage the pace.

## Price and Trend

USD/CNY peaked at 7.1400 on 14 October 2025 and has fallen almost without interruption since, reaching 6.6954 on 21 September. The 50-day average (6.7206) is well below the 200-day (6.8204). In the past month, however, the pair has moved sideways (−0.1 per cent), consistent with the central bank’s weaker fixings. The z-score is −1.3 and the RSI 37, neither extreme.

## Volatility

One-month realised volatility of 0.9 per cent is in the 2nd percentile of the past ten years and far below the median of 3.2 per cent. One-year volatility is 2.1 per cent. These numbers reflect the management of the currency more than market calm, and they mean that small moves in the renminbi carry a clear policy message.

## Distribution of Returns

The one-day value at risk at 95 per cent is only −0.22 per cent, but excess kurtosis of 3.5 shows that the rare larger moves are more frequent than a normal distribution would imply. The largest fall of the year was −0.58 per cent on 7 April and the largest rise +0.40 per cent on 13 March. A variance ratio of 0.84 points to some mean reversion from day to day, consistent with the central bank smoothing moves.

## What Moves the Pair

Over the past year, weekly changes in USD/CNY have been most closely linked to the VIX (+0.51) and to US equities (−0.41), as well as to the broad dollar (+0.65). In other words, the renminbi weakens when global markets are stressed and strengthens when they are calm. The link with interest rates is weak (+0.25 with the US two-year yield), because capital controls limit the flows that would normally respond to yield gaps.

## Macro and Policy Context

China’s economy is growing at 4.3 per cent with inflation of only 0.8 per cent and weak retail sales, so monetary policy remains loose: the five-year loan prime rate is 3.5 per cent and Chinese two-year yields are 1.26 per cent. The appreciation of the renminbi has come amid political pressure on China’s currency, including from the European Union, which argues that it is undervalued. President Xi’s visit to Washington from 23 to 25 September produced no trade breakthrough, and further meetings are expected at the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November.

## Risk Considerations and Calendar

The main risk to a stronger renminbi is a breakdown in trade talks with the United States, which could lead the central bank to allow weakness, or a sharp rise in global risk aversion.
