## Executive Summary

**The weekend added risk, not yet lost barrels.** None of the Houthi claims on Yanbu, Rabigh or the East-West pipeline has been confirmed by Saudi Arabia or Aramco. Kpler data show Saudi crude exports of 5.5 million barrels a day in the first 25 days of October, and Yanbu loadings of 2.2 million.

**The system has no shock absorber.** The IEA puts usable spare capacity at 0.27 million barrels a day, most of it trapped behind Hormuz. Global inventories are 509 million barrels below end-February, and the US strategic reserve is at its lowest since 1982. Any confirmed outage would be priced at once.

**Gas is the bigger problem for Europe.** Qatar’s LNG exports have almost stopped since March, TTF prices have tripled and EU storage is about fourteen points below normal going into winter.

**Our view.** We expect Brent to stay between $100 and $120 into year-end, with risks skewed higher until Hormuz traffic normalises. Inflation keeps central banks on a tightening path.

## The Weekend Attacks

Between 24 and 26 October Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed strikes on an Aramco facility at Yanbu, the Rabigh refinery, crude pumping stations and the East-West pipeline, as well as airports and an air base. The Saudi-led coalition reported intercepting several ballistic missiles, drones and a cruise missile. On 27 October the Houthis claimed a missile had set fire to the Ras Tanura refinery, which processes about 550,000 barrels a day; Bloomberg, citing a person familiar with the matter, reported that a fire near the site had been contained. The extent of any damage is unknown.

**What is claimed and what is confirmed:**

- **Aramco, Yanbu**: Houthi strike claim, 24 Oct. Confirmed: missiles intercepted; no damage reported.
- **Rabigh refinery**: Houthi claim, 24 Oct. Confirmed: no statement.
- **East-West pipeline pumping stations**: Houthi claims, 24 and 26 Oct. Confirmed: no statement.
- **Ras Tanura**: Houthi claim, refinery hit, 27 Oct. Confirmed: fire nearby contained (Bloomberg); 3 missiles intercepted.
- **Riyadh, Khamis Mushait**: missile and drone attacks. Confirmed: interceptions reported by coalition.

Sources: ABC; Newsquawk; FinanceFeeds; Bloomberg; KFGO/Reuters; Saudi-led coalition statements. Claims are unverified.

The market has seen this before. On 10 September drones disabled several pumping stations on the East-West pipeline, launched from Iraq according to Saudi Arabia; Yanbu loadings stopped for eleven days and Brent rose more than $3 in a session. The pipeline is now the main route around Hormuz, which makes it the most valuable target in the region.

## Hormuz and the Bypass Routes

Before the war about 20.9 million barrels a day of crude and products passed through the Strait of Hormuz, a fifth of world consumption. The IEA estimates that 10.9 million passed in September, with another 5.6 million on bypass routes: the Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu and the UAE’s Habshan-Fujairah line. Daily tracking is erratic, because many tankers sail with transponders off, and at least 28 attacks or security incidents were recorded in the first half of October.

## The Supply Balance

The IEA has described the shock as the largest supply disruption in the history of the oil market: world output fell by 10.1 million barrels a day in March, about 9% of supply. Supply has partly recovered to 102.3 million barrels a day in September, but the 2026 average is 5.8 million below last year, while demand is down only 2.6 million. The gap has been filled from inventories, which are now 509 million barrels below end-February.

## Prices and the Curve

Brent’s highest close this year was $118.03 on 29 April, and it briefly traded near $126 intraday the following day. The curve tells the same story as the inventories: the gap between the first two Brent contracts is about $5 a barrel, in the 99th percentile since 1990, according to MUFG. Refined products are tighter still. US heating oil futures are up more than 130% this year, and the refining margin on a typical barrel reached its highest since 2006 in September.

Source: ICE and NYMEX front-month futures via Yahoo Finance, to 26 October 2026.

## Natural Gas

For Europe the gas market is the sharper risk. Qatar halted LNG production on 2 March and shipped 18 cargoes in the first six months of the war, against 509 a year earlier. The Dutch TTF benchmark touched €97.50 per megawatt-hour on 26 October, its highest since December 2022, and EU storage was 74.6% full, about fourteen points below the 2017-25 average. Asian LNG prices are near $30 per million British thermal units.

Source: ICE Endex via Yahoo Finance, to 26 October 2026.

## Inflation and Central Banks

The shock has reached consumer prices. US inflation was 3.7% in September with energy up 20.4%; the euro area reached 3.8% with energy up 18.9%; the UK rose to 3.5% with motor fuels up 29.8%. The Dallas Fed estimates that a three-quarter Hormuz closure adds about 1.1 points to US headline PCE inflation but only 0.3 to the core. Central banks have chosen to lean against second-round effects: the Fed raised rates in September and the ECB in June and September.

- **Euro area**: CPI 3.8%; energy +18.9%; 2.50% (deposit); hold expected 29 Oct.

Households are feeling it. The University of Michigan’s sentiment index fell to 48.0 in October, the second-lowest reading in 74 years, and one-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7%. Higher rates and lower real incomes raise the risk of recession in 2027, especially in energy-importing economies.
