## Executive Summary

**A strong trend, now overbought.** The pair is 2.0 per cent above its 50-day average and 2.9 per cent above its 200-day, with a z-score of +2.0 and an RSI of 79, the highest in this series. The trend is clear, but in the short term the move is stretched.

**Oil no longer drives the loonie.** The correlation of weekly changes with WTI crude over the past year is only −0.07, against about −0.4 between 2014 and 2019 on our calculation. The Canadian dollar is trading on interest rates and on trade policy, not on energy.

**Range.** The one-month one-sigma range is 1.4082 to 1.4443. MUFG expects 1.42 at the end of the year, close to the current level, and 1.36 by the third quarter of 2027.

## Price and Trend

USD/CAD fell to 1.3524 on 29 January and has risen gradually since, with an acceleration in September when the Fed raised rates. The 50-day average (1.3975) is above the 200-day (1.3865), the standard sign of an upward trend, and the price is at the top of its 52-week range. The variance ratio of 1.27 is the highest in this series, which means weekly moves are larger than daily moves would suggest: a trending market.

## Volatility

One-month realised volatility of 2.3 per cent compares with a ten-year median of 6.1 per cent, and three-month volatility is 4.4 per cent. The pair has risen in small, regular steps, with few large days. Such low volatility can persist, but it often ends when a scheduled event, here the Bank of Canada decision and Monetary Policy Report on 28 October, forces a repricing.

Source: Andra Markets calculation from ECB reference rates: 21-day standard deviation of daily log returns, annualised with √252. The median is over ten years.

## Distribution of Returns

Daily changes are small: the one-day value at risk at 95 per cent is −0.43 per cent, the lowest of the G10 pairs here apart from the managed yuan. The largest fall of the year was −0.96 per cent on 3 September and the largest rise +0.89 per cent on 2 February. The skew of −0.39 indicates that falls in the pair, when they come, tend to be slightly sharper than rises.

## What Moves the Pair

Weekly changes in USD/CAD show a correlation of +0.69 with the broad dollar and +0.30 with the US two-year yield over the past year, and +0.32 with the gap between US and euro area two-year yields. The link with oil, the traditional driver, is essentially zero. One explanation is trade policy: since the United States declined to renew the USMCA at the 1 July joint review, the agreement remains in force but under annual reviews, and Canadian exports face uncertainty that higher oil prices do not offset.

## Macro and Policy Context

The Bank of Canada held its rate at 2.25 per cent on 2 September while noting upside risks to inflation from energy and tariffs. Canadian inflation is 3.0 per cent but measures of underlying inflation are close to 2 per cent, and unemployment is 6.4 per cent, so the case for higher rates is weaker than in the United States. Both central banks decide on 28 October; a Bank of Canada increase, which some forecasters now expect, would be the main catalyst for a lower USD/CAD.

## Risk Considerations and Calendar

The main risk to a higher dollar is a hawkish Bank of Canada on 28 October or a Fed pause. Positive news on trade with the United States would also support the loonie, while new tariffs would weaken it further. Key dates: Canadian jobs on 9 October, Canadian CPI and the Business Outlook Survey on 19 October, and the Bank of Canada and the FOMC on 28 October. A record-low volatility move into a double central bank day is the setup to watch.
