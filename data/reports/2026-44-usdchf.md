## Executive Summary

**A clean trend.** The pair is 2.2 per cent above its 50-day average and 4.6 per cent above its 200-day, and both averages are rising. Momentum and carry point the same way. This is the strongest trend among the six pairs in this series.

**But the move is stretched.** The price is at 98 per cent of its 52-week range and +2.4 standard deviations above its one-year average. On our data since 2014, the pair fell in the following month in four of the five non-overlapping periods that began with a z-score above +2, by 2.8 per cent on average, although the sample is small.

**Range.** The one-month one-sigma range is 0.8187 to 0.8490. MUFG sees 0.848 by the end of the year but 0.784 by the third quarter of 2027, a sign that the franc’s weakness is seen as cyclical.

## Price and Trend

USD/CHF fell to 0.7670 on 10 February, its lowest level since at least 2014 on our data, and has since risen almost 9 per cent. The rise accelerated after the Fed’s increase on 16 September. Unlike the other dollar pairs, it has not been interrupted by large reversals: the largest one-year drawdown was −5.3 per cent. The RSI of 64 shows strength without being overbought.

## What Moves the Pair

Over the past year USD/CHF has been more sensitive to interest rates than the other pairs: the correlation of weekly changes with the US two-year yield is +0.33 and with euro area two-year yields +0.34, because Swiss rates are anchored at zero and every rise abroad widens the gap. The correlation with the broad dollar is +0.72. The pair also rises with oil (+0.21) and falls when US equities rise (−0.26), a sign that part of the dollar’s strength reflects geopolitical risk rather than pure carry.

## Risk Considerations and Calendar

The main risks to a higher dollar are a sell-off in equities, an escalation in the Middle East or European fiscal stress, all of which typically strengthen the franc, and a pause by the Fed. On the other side, the SNB’s willingness to intervene limits the franc’s strength. Key dates: US CPI on 14 October, the FOMC on 28 October and the SNB on 10 December. The trend is strong, but it is a trend in a safe-haven currency, which can reverse in a day.
