**Coverage summary.** Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) manufactures the most advanced chips in the world for companies that design but do not make them, including Nvidia, Apple, AMD and Broadcom. In the second quarter its revenue rose 36 per cent to NT$1.27 trillion (US$40.2 billion), net income rose 77 per cent and the gross margin reached 67.7 per cent. Monthly sales published on 8 October show third-quarter revenue of about NT$1.49 trillion, which on our calculation is above the top of the company’s guidance. TSMC raised its 2026 capital spending plan to US$60 to 64 billion and expects revenue growth of slightly more than 40 per cent this year. It reports third-quarter results on 15 October. The Taiwan-listed shares reached a record on 5 October and are up more than 55 per cent this year.

## Executive Summary

**Ahead of its own guidance.** TSMC guided third-quarter revenue of US$44.6 to 45.8 billion. Sales for July, August and September add up to NT$1,494 billion, about US$46.7 billion at the exchange rate assumed in the guidance, which would be above the top of the range, although the result in dollars depends on the actual exchange rate. September sales were 55 per cent higher than a year earlier.

**AI has taken over the business.** High-performance computing, the category that includes AI accelerators and data centre processors, accounted for 66 per cent of revenue in the second quarter, against 22 per cent for smartphones. Chief executive C.C. Wei said demand for AI accelerators is getting “stronger and stronger”, and TSMC’s plans for spending in the next three years are significantly higher than in the past three.

**Margins will dip from a record.** The gross margin is guided to fall to 65 to 67 per cent in the third quarter, as the ramp of the new 2-nanometre process dilutes it by 3 to 4 points and the fabs in Arizona, Japan and Germany cost more to run than those in Taiwan. At about 20 times forward earnings, the shares trade at a discount to most chip designers despite growing about as fast.

## Business Overview

TSMC is a foundry: it does not design or sell chips under its own name, but manufactures them for other companies, which allows it to serve competitors such as Nvidia and AMD at the same time. Its advantage lies in the most advanced manufacturing processes, measured in nanometres, where it has no real competitor at scale. Processes of 7 nanometres or smaller generated 77 per cent of wafer revenue in the second quarter, led by 5 nanometres at 33 per cent and 3 nanometres at 30 per cent, while the new 2-nanometre process contributed 3 per cent in its first quarter of volume.

TSMC also leads in advanced packaging, the technology (called CoWoS) that joins processors with high-bandwidth memory to build AI accelerators. Capacity is so tight that Wei said it limits customers’ growth and that TSMC welcomes alternatives, including Intel’s. Reports in the Taiwanese press point to capacity doubling by 2028. As Wei put it on the July call, choosing and ramping a technology “is not buying a milk from 7-Eleven”.

## Second-Quarter Results

Revenue rose 36 per cent in Taiwan dollars and 33.7 per cent in US dollars, and net income rose 77 per cent to NT$707 billion, as higher prices for advanced processes and full factories lifted margins. The gross margin of 67.7 per cent and the operating margin of 60.3 per cent are among the highest of any manufacturing company in the world. Earnings per ADR, the US-listed share, were US$4.31.

## Third Quarter So Far

TSMC publishes revenue every month, which gives an early read on the quarter. July revenue was NT$467.6 billion, up 45 per cent from a year earlier, August NT$514.8 billion, up 53 per cent, and September NT$511.9 billion, up 55 per cent. In total, the third quarter came to about NT$1,494 billion. At the exchange rate of 32 Taiwan dollars per US dollar assumed in the guidance, that is about US$46.7 billion, above the top of the US$44.6 to 45.8 billion range. The beat looks likely; the open question is the margin, given the cost of ramping 2 nanometres.

## Expansion and Costs

TSMC is investing heavily outside Taiwan, partly to reduce geopolitical risk and to avoid US tariffs. In July it committed another US$100 billion to Arizona for at least four more fabs at 2 nanometres and below, bringing its announced US investment to US$265 billion. Its fab in Dresden, Germany, celebrated its topping-out in September and is on schedule, and in Japan it is forming an image-sensor joint venture with Sony. These fabs cost more to run: management expects them to reduce the gross margin by 2 to 3 points at first, and by 3 to 4 points later. To protect margins, TSMC has reportedly told customers it will raise some prices by up to 10 per cent in 2027, which the company has not confirmed.

## Valuation

At US$472.20, TSMC’s ADR values the company at US$2.08 trillion, about 30 times trailing earnings and 20.4 times forward earnings, below the multiples of Broadcom, AMD and ASML and similar to Nvidia’s. The 21 analysts covered by StockAnalysis rate it a strong buy, with an average target of US$555, 17.5 per cent above the current price. The shares trade at a discount to the chip designers they supply largely because of the political risk attached to Taiwan.

## Risk Considerations

The most discussed risk is geopolitical: most of TSMC’s advanced capacity is in Taiwan, and tensions with China, including frequent military flights near the island, would make any disruption extremely costly for the whole technology industry. Currency is a second risk, since a stronger Taiwan dollar reduces the value of dollar sales; the central bank intervened in July to slow its rise. Third, US tariffs on imported chips, at 25 per cent on certain advanced chips since January, may be broadened, although companies building in the United States are expected to be exempt. Finally, TSMC depends on a few customers: its largest, reported to be Nvidia, accounted for 19 per cent of revenue in 2025, and an AI spending slowdown would hit it with a lag, once its US$60 billion-plus capacity plan is already committed.

## Outlook and Monitoring Points

TSMC reports third-quarter results on 15 October. Beyond the likely revenue beat, investors will focus on the gross margin given the 2-nanometre ramp, guidance for the fourth quarter, any first comments on capital spending and growth in 2027, and updates on advanced packaging capacity and pricing. Because TSMC makes chips for almost every AI company, its comments are a read-across for Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom, which report later.
