## Executive Summary

**No trend, but a strong carry.** The pair sits almost exactly on its 50-day and 200-day averages (+0.4 and −0.1 per cent), so trend models give no signal. What supports the dollar is carry: the two-year yield gap of 2.88 percentage points pays an investor about 2.9 per cent a year to hold dollars against yen, and twelve-month momentum is slightly positive.

**The risk is in the left tail.** Daily returns over the past year show a skew of −1.60 and excess kurtosis of 6.8. In plain terms, the pair tends to rise slowly and fall suddenly. The five largest daily moves of the year were all falls, four of them of more than 2 per cent, including the intervention in early August.

**Statistical range.** With three-month volatility of 9.2 per cent, the one-standard-deviation range for the next month is about 154.1 to 162.6. The upper end overlaps the area where Japan intervened in July, which makes a move above 162 statistically possible but politically difficult.

## Price and Trend

Over the past two years USD/JPY has moved inside a broad 140 to 165 band. In 2026 it rose to 163.91 on 28 July, then fell to 156.68 by 3 August, about 4 per cent in three sessions, after a joint intervention by Japan and the United States, confirmed by the Ministry of Finance on 3 August. Since then it has climbed back steadily. ## Macro and Policy Context

Both central banks are raising rates. The Fed lifted its target range to 3.75 to 4.00 per cent on 16 September and the median projection shows one more increase this year; markets price about an 80 per cent chance of a move in December. The Bank of Japan raised its rate to 1.25 per cent on 18 September by a vote of seven to two, with two members preferring a slower pace. MUFG expects the next BoJ increase in December and a path to 2 per cent by mid-2027. The yield gap therefore depends on the relative pace of the two banks, and on Japanese fiscal policy, which under Prime Minister Takaichi remains expansionary.

## Positioning and Forecasts

Speculative investors remain long the yen: CFTC data show a net long position of 55,440 contracts in the week to 29 September, down from 71,982 the week before, so part of the bet on a stronger yen is being reduced as the pair rises. Forecasters expect a gradual decline: MUFG sees 155 at the end of the year and 149 by the third quarter of 2027, while the August Reuters poll had 159 at three months and 154 at twelve months. These forecasts assume that the BoJ keeps raising rates and that the Fed stops after December.

## Risk Considerations and Calendar

The main risk to a higher dollar is a second intervention if the pair approaches the July highs; Japan showed in July that it is prepared to act, and with US support. A faster BoJ, a pause by the Fed after weak US jobs data (payrolls rose only 29,000 in September) or a sell-off in global equities, during which the yen usually strengthens, would also push the pair lower. On the other side, a December Fed hike and Japanese fiscal expansion would support it. Key dates: US CPI on 14 October, Japanese CPI on 22 October, the FOMC on 28 October and the BoJ on 30 October. The numbers favour the dollar; the policy risk favours the yen.
