**Coverage summary.** Alphabet posted revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25%, Microsoft $92.3 billion, up 19%, and Meta $64.6 billion, up 26%. The price action did not follow: Alphabet fell 10.4% after hours on 28 October, Meta lost 7.0% in the session after its 21 October report and Microsoft rose around 4% after hours. The difference is cash. Alphabet’s free cash flow turned negative at minus $9.1 billion as capex more than doubled, while Microsoft kept free cash flow positive.

## Executive Summary

**Demand is not in question.** Google Cloud grew 103% and Azure 48%, accelerating from 82% and 43% the quarter before. Search queries grew at their fastest pace in five years, and Meta guided fourth-quarter revenue to $73 to 76 billion.

**The cash bridge is.** Alphabet’s operating cash flow of $42.3 billion did not cover $51.4 billion of capex. Meta’s capex of $34.9 billion exceeded operating cash flow of $32.8 billion. Microsoft spent $45.2 billion including finance leases, below the roughly $50.7 billion analysts expected, and remained free cash flow positive.

**Spending keeps rising.** Capex now absorbs between 40% and 54% of quarterly revenue at the three companies. The midpoints of their 2026 guidance add up to roughly $515 billion, before Amazon reports tonight, and none has signalled that 2027 will be lower.

**Our reading.** The market has moved from rewarding AI ambition to pricing who can fund it from operating cash. We expect that filter to dominate how the group trades until 2027 capex plans are known.

## Alphabet

Operationally, Alphabet had an outstanding quarter. Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion, a record, with sales of TPU systems contributing meaningfully alongside enterprise AI infrastructure. Search and other grew 12%, which addresses the fear that AI answers cannibalise search. Operating income rose 30% to $40.6 billion and the operating margin reached 31.8%. The weak spot was YouTube advertising, where growth slowed to 9% at $11.2 billion, according to Variety.

Earnings were messier. EPS of $2.12 missed the $3.02 LSEG consensus, partly because of an $8.7 billion net loss on equity securities that includes an $8.2 billion unrealized loss on SpaceX. CNBC reported that adjusted EPS of $2.67 also fell short, Alphabet’s first combined revenue and earnings miss in nearly four years.

## Microsoft

Microsoft delivered what the market wanted. Revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion and operating income 21% to $45.9 billion. Azure grew 48%, ahead of the roughly 45% guidance and up from 43% the previous quarter. At $33.2 billion it is now Microsoft’s largest single business, about 36% of revenue, and Microsoft Cloud reached $63.9 billion, up 30%. The commercial backlog stands at about $677 billion.

Capex of $45.2 billion rose 30% and came in below expectations. One caveat matters for comparisons: from fiscal 2027 Microsoft extended the useful life of data centres from 15 to 25 years and is moving more future data centre leases from finance to operating leases. That lowers reported capex and flatters free cash flow without changing what is being built, although about two thirds of spending goes to short-lived servers that are unaffected.

Source: cloud growth compares Google Cloud in Q2 and Q3 2026 and Azure in Microsoft fiscal Q4 2026 and fiscal Q1 2027; Alphabet 8-K; Reuters via The Star, 31 Jul 2026; Hargreaves Lansdown, 30 Jul 2026; Investing.com, 28 Oct 2026.

## Meta

Meta’s revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion, beating the $63.4 billion consensus cited by CNBC. Costs, however, rose 68% to $51.6 billion, including a $9.89 billion legal charge. Excluding it, operating income would have been $22.9 billion, a margin near 35% by our calculation, still below 40% a year ago. Capex of $34.9 billion exceeded operating cash flow, and Meta gave no 2027 capex figure.
