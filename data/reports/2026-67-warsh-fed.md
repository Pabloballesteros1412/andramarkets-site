**Coverage summary.** The Federal Open Market Committee kept the federal funds target range at 3.75% to 4.00% by ten votes to two; Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack preferred a quarter-point increase. The statement upgraded growth to “strong” and said the “accumulation of more information” would guide the next step. Chair Kevin Warsh then announced that in 2027 one of the eight scheduled meetings will be devoted to strategy, with no rate decision, and two in 2028. Markets put the probability of a December hike at 80% to 90%.

## Executive Summary

**A pause with a tightening bias.** The Committee held after September’s hike, but two dissents for an immediate increase and a statement that calls growth “strong” point towards December. Most economists, including those at Goldman Sachs Asset Management and Fitch, expect a hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting.

**Inflation is the problem, mostly energy.** Headline CPI was 3.7% in September, driven by the oil shock, while core CPI was 2.6%. The Fed is leaning against second-round effects, even with job growth close to stalling: payrolls rose only 29,000 in September.

**Fewer rate decisions.** Warsh keeps eight meetings a year but turns one into a strategy session in 2027 and two in 2028. It is the first change to the number of rate-setting meetings since 1981 and fits his rejection of forward guidance and “data dependence”.

**Our view.** We expect a quarter-point hike in December to 4.00% to 4.25%, after which the Fed pauses if oil stabilises. The bond market is the risk: the ten-year yield is back around 5%.

## The Decision

The statement was short, as is now customary under Warsh. It said economic activity “is expanding at a strong pace”, that productivity growth is strong and capital investment robust, and that “inflation remains elevated”. The key sentence replaced September’s justification for a hike: “The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee’s 2 percent goal.” Reuters noted it reads like the forward guidance Warsh says he avoids.

**FOMC decisions under Chair Warsh:**

- **17 Jun**: hold; 3.50-3.75%; unanimous; no dissents
- **29 Jul**: hold; 3.50-3.75%; 9-3; Hammack, Logan, Kashkari (hike)
- **16 Sep**: +25bp; 3.75-4.00%; 12-0; no dissents
- **28 Oct**: hold; 3.75-4.00%; 10-2; Cook, Hammack (hike)
- **8-9 Dec**: hike priced 80-90%; 4.00-4.25%?; SEP and dot plot due

Sources: Federal Reserve; Reuters; Yahoo Finance; KPMG. December pricing from fed funds futures and Polymarket, 28 October.

The dissents matter as signals. Hammack has now dissented for a hike twice since July, her third hawkish dissent this year. Cook’s vote was her first dissent as a governor; she remains in office after the Supreme Court ruled in June that she can stay while the President’s attempt to remove her is litigated. A governor under political pressure voting for higher rates is a strong statement of independence.

## Inflation and Jobs

Inflation re-accelerated after the war with Iran began in February. Headline CPI rose from 2.4% in February to 4.2% in May, eased in the summer and returned to 3.7% in September, with energy prices up 20.4%. Core CPI has stayed between 2.4% and 2.9%, but core PCE, the Fed’s preferred gauge, was 3.0% in August. The Fed’s concern is that energy costs feed into wages and expectations; one-year consumer expectations reached 4.7% in October.

Source: BLS and BEA via FRED (CPIAUCSL, CPILFESL, PCEPILFE), monthly; CPI to September, core PCE to August 2026.

The labour market is the counter-argument. Payrolls rose 29,000 in September, July was revised to a fall of 10,000 after a drop of 156,000 in February, and average hourly earnings are growing only 3.0% a year. Unemployment is still low at 4.2%, and the Fed’s statement says job gains “have kept pace with the workforce”. Weak hiring with low unemployment reflects slower labour-force growth as much as weak demand.

Source: BLS via FRED (PAYEMS), seasonally adjusted, as revised in the September report.

## Rates and the Bond Market

Markets have repriced sharply this year. The two-year Treasury yield has risen about 1.3 points since December to 4.78%, tracking the expected path of policy, and the ten-year traded near 5.04% after the decision. The term premium, the extra yield investors demand to hold long bonds, is close to 1%. Mortgage rates are back above 7%, which tightens financial conditions without any further move by the Fed, a point Warsh acknowledged: conditions have tightened “for some businesses and households, much as we would expect”.

Source: Federal Reserve via FRED (DFEDTARU, DGS2, DGS10), to 27 October 2026; daily yields sampled weekly.

## The Meeting Schedule

Since 1981 the FOMC has met eight times a year; in the 1950s and 1960s it met as often as nineteen times. Warsh said “eight meetings per year is the right number”, but that in 2027 one will be dedicated to strategy, with “neither a vote on the federal funds rate nor a policy statement anticipated”, and two in 2028. His rationale: policy should be set “at timely, regular, and information-rich intervals”, leaving time for “thinking anew about big questions”. The new schedule takes effect from next autumn; what will be published after strategy meetings is still undecided.

The change is less radical than it sounds: the ECB, the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan moved to eight meetings a decade ago. But fewer decision dates mean larger moves, or longer waits, when conditions change quickly. With no forward guidance and fewer meetings, each decision carries more information, and markets are likely to react more strongly on the day.

- **2027 (Warsh)**: 7

## The Warsh Agenda

**Warsh’s institutional agenda, stated view and action so far:**

- **Forward guidance**: only in a crisis; shorter statements, no explicit guidance
- **Data dependence**: little value in decimals; focus on trends, fewer decision meetings
- **Balance sheet**: “bloated”, can be cut significantly; $6.75tn, still ample reserves, working group due Q1 2027
- **Dot plot**: sceptical; still published, Warsh submits no dot
- **Independence**: politics played “no role”; hike in September despite White House pressure

Sources: Warsh speeches (Hoover Institution, 2025) and press conference, 28 October 2026; FRED (WALCL); Chase; 404K Research (secondary).

Warsh’s most radical idea, a much smaller balance sheet, is still on hold. Total assets are $6.75tn, a quarter below the 2022 peak but slightly higher than at the end of 2025, because the Fed buys Treasury bills to keep reserves ample. Any shrinkage is unlikely before the working group reports in early 2027.

Source: Federal Reserve H.4.1 via FRED (WALCL), weekly sampled monthly, to 21 October 2026. Peak $8.97tn in April 2022.
